Install in R

Download the source package above, then choose that .tar.gz file when prompted. Both functions use base R and its standard stats package.

install.packages(file.choose(), repos = NULL, type = "source")
library(causalgap)
?precision_share
?aipw_u

Calculate the internal-control share

Suppose half the records come from the trial, two thirds of trial participants receive treatment, and the two control variances are equal at a given covariate profile. One sixth of all records are internal controls, while one half are external controls. The internal share of the control comparison is one quarter.

precision_share(p = 0.5, e = 2/3, v_internal = 1, v_external = 1)
# [1] 0.25

Reproduce the EIF lab in R

Download the worked R example and source it after installing the package. The script enumerates a small synthetic population exactly. The calculations have no simulation error.

source("hybrid-eif.R")
fit <- hybrid_example(delta = 0.2, external_variance = 1)
round(fit$estimate, 3)
#   tce trial   gap
#  1.15  1.00  0.15

mean(fit$influence$tce^2)
# [1] 4.528125

The external control mean is 0.2 lower than the internal control mean at each covariate value. The 75% external share therefore shifts the contrast upward by 0.15. The last number is the variance bound per record for the borrowing target under this law. For 300 independent records, the corresponding asymptotic standard error is about 0.123.

Now set delta = 0. The gap is zero, but in a model that allows unequal control means, a small change in either source can open it. The EIF lab shows why this distinction matters for its uncertainty.

Using fitted predictions

precision_share()
Computes the internal share from the source probability, trial treatment probability and two conditional control variances.
aipw_u()
Accepts observed source, treatment and outcome and one row of nuisance predictions per record. Returns the borrowing contrast, matched trial contrast, their difference and the influence values. The seven signed contributions are available separately.

The package evaluates the scores; supply your own fitted outcome means, variances and source probabilities. For the model fitting, bootstrap and simulations used in the paper, use the replication guide. Clinical records are obtained separately under the data distributor’s terms.
