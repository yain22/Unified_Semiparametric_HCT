Choosing a control arm

Investigators plan to treat 120 children and want 80% power to detect a two-point improvement in negative symptoms of schizophrenia. Historical controls could reduce the number of children assigned to concurrent control. How much disagreement between the two control sources would the design tolerate?

How the calculation works

Each arm has marginal SD 7.5. The known prognostic function explains 25% of outcome variation, so the common residual variance is v = 0.75 × 7.5². If a design uses m total controls, its planning variance is v(1/120 + 1/m). Thus the RCT row is the paper’s ANCOVA reference.

For HCT the internal share λ is the internal control count divided by the total control count. Its causal-gap ξ is (1 − λ)δ. The RCT gap is zero and the ECT gap is δ. Under a true trial effect θ, the one-sided test declares benefit with probability Φ((θ + ξ)/SE − 1.960), where Φ is the standard normal distribution function.

These Gaussian planning calculations treat variances and borrowing shares as known. To see how learning a borrowing share from the data changes sampling uncertainty, explore the efficient influence function.
